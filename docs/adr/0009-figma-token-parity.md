# Design tokens flow Figma → tokens.json → retro.css, and a test fails on drift

The owner made the Figma Brand Book (file `tamehh67b8zUgteY6xVQv5`) the design source of truth on 2026-09-10 ("implement everything that is in Figma"). Until then `site/retro.css` carried only fifteen of the twenty primitive colours; none of the semantic Color tokens, spacing, radius, elevation, motion, typography or type-scale variables existed in CSS, and 26 hard-coded hexes plus a leftover green glow lived outside the token block, so Figma and the site could drift silently. We now keep all eight Figma variable collections in `retro.css` under the exact Figma WEB code-syntax names: primitives are the only place a colour literal may appear; semantic tokens are `var()` aliases of primitives, with the Color collection's Dark mode in `:root` and its Light mode redefined only inside `.inner-page main` (the long-form content area — headers and footers stay Dark). `site/tokens.json` is a committed export of the collections, and `internal/site/tokens_test.go` parses the stylesheet, resolves every alias chain and fails `go test ./...` if a resolved value differs from the export or a colour literal appears outside the token blocks. A handful of overlay and glow tokens that Figma cannot express (translucent washes) are allowed but marked "derived, not in Figma" in the export. Consequence: a token change is a two-file change (Figma export and stylesheet) in one commit; a shipped value may override Figma only when the Figma variable is updated to match and the reason is recorded (the 3px focus ring is the one such case).
